# Wireframes

Generated by [`../scripts/wireframe.py`](../scripts/wireframe.py) from
[`../fixtures/sessions.json`](../fixtures/sessions.json). Do not hand-edit —
regenerate:

```bash
python3 scripts/wireframe.py --all --png
```

Both formats are checked in. **SVG is the artefact**; the PNG is a preview at 110
DPI for viewing in a browser or a diff. The SVG is in millimetres at true physical
size, so it is the one to print.

## How to read one

Each sheet renders the *same* simulated sessions into whatever pane the candidate
panel gives, wrapped and clipped exactly as a terminal would.

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Header, right | `READABLE` / `TOO SMALL` — does the pane meet the tier it is scored against |
| Header, line 2 | Achieved cells per pane, the tier target, and the Hack point size implied |
| **Red `+N↑`** | Lines wrapping pushed off the top of that pane. The real cost of narrow panes |
| **Red pane border** | Session `blocked` on a permission prompt |
| **Green pane border + ✓** | Session `done` |
| Footer | The distance to view from, at 100% and scaled to A4 |

Scaling note, because it is the thing that makes a 1200 mm panel testable on a
desk: scaling the sheet by *k* and viewing it from *k* × the stated distance
preserves angular character height **exactly**, so the readability verdict is
unchanged. Each sheet prints its own A4-equivalent distance.

### Does the observed pane size scale to eight?

Scored against the `observed` tier (26 × 39) at 700 mm.

| File | Panel | Cells/pane | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `8x1-32in-4k-observed` | 32″ 4K | 30 × 65 | **READABLE** |
| `8x1-40in-5k2k-observed` | 40″ 21:9 5K2K | 40 × 65 | READABLE |
| `8x1-49in-32x9-observed` | 49″ 32:9 DQHD | 52 × 52 | READABLE |

**This is the important result.** Eight sessions in one row at the pane size
already proven in daily use fits a **standard 32″ 4K**, with 65 rows against the 39
in use today — 26 rows of headroom per pane. The wireframe shows the panes are not
even full.

The case for a 40″ or 49″ panel is therefore not *pane count*. It is columns per
pane: 30 → 40 → 52. Whether that is worth the money is the open question, and it
reduces to whether 26–30 columns is a tolerance or a preference.

| `6x1-32in-4k` | 32″ 4K | 6 × 1 | 40 × 65 | TOO SMALL |

Two shapes worth looking at rather than reading:

- **`1x6-32in-4k`** — six panes stacked as rows gives 249 columns and **10 rows**.
  Absurd in the other direction, and the clearest demonstration that stacking
  spends the scarce axis.
- **`4x2-49in-32x9`** — 105 columns, 25 rows. Fails on rows despite having the most
  columns of anything scored. This is the 32:9 trap, and it only appears when panes
  are stacked.

### Vertical, square and TV-class form factors

Added 2026-08-12. Scored against the `observed` tier (26 × 39) at 700 mm. Pane
grids are not chosen — each is the maximum that panel yields.

| File | Panel | PPI | Panes | Cells/pane |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `3x2-24in-rotated` | 24″ 1080p **rotated** | 93 | 3 × 2 | 33 × 42 |
| `4x2-27in-4k-rotated` | 27″ 4K **rotated** | 163 | 4 × 2 | 28 × 50 |
| `5x2-32in-4k-rotated` | 32″ 4K **rotated** | 140 | 5 × 2 | 26 × 59 |
| `5x2-32in-signage-vertical` | 32″ 9:16 vertical-**native** signage | 70 | 5 × 2 | 26 × 56 |
| `6x2-28in-dualup` | 27.6″ 16:18 LG DualUp (native) | 140 | 6 × 2 | 26 × 43 |
| `6x1-26in-square` | 26.5″ 1:1 square (Eizo class) | 102 | 6 × 1 | 27 × 77 |
| `13x2-48in-tv` | 48″ 4K TV class | 92 | 13 × 2 | 27 × 48 |

**What these renders show that the pane counts do not.** Every vertical and square
configuration produces panes that are *far taller than a session needs and
narrower than one wants*. The square panel is the extreme: **27 columns and 77
rows**, when the measured working session uses 39 rows. Roughly half that pane is
empty in the render, and it is empty because the content ran out, not because the
sheet was cropped.

The direct comparison is the same panel with and without rotation:

| 32″ 4K | Panes | Cells/pane |
|---|---|---|
| landscape, `8x1-32in-4k-observed` | 8 | 30 × 65 |
| rotated, `5x2-32in-4k-rotated` | 10 | 26 × 59 |

Rotating buys **two extra panes and costs four columns each** — and on top of that
it breaks subpixel text rendering, because ClearType and FreeType LCD filtering
both assume horizontal RGB stripes. Verified on this machine: `fc-match` reports
`rgba: 1` (horizontal RGB) and nothing anywhere sets `vrgb`. That penalty applies
to the *rotated* rows only; the vertical-**native** signage panel does not pay it,
but pays 70 PPI instead, which is the coarsest glyph rendering of anything scored.

Not rendered: the 8.8″ and 29″ bar displays. At 700 mm a 1920×540 bar yields 23
usable rows and a 1920×480 yields 8, both below the 40 one pane needs. A bar is a
status strip — one line per session — not a pane device.

## Adding a configuration

Append to `CONFIGS` in [`../scripts/wireframe.py`](../scripts/wireframe.py):

```python
"key": ("label", diagonal_in, aspect_w, aspect_h, px_w, px_h,
        grid_cols, grid_rows, tier, distance_override_or_None),
```

Then regenerate. Keep the generated files in the commit — they are the point, and
a checked-in render makes a model change show up as a reviewable visual diff.
